Lesson Introduction

Communication is something we do all day long — we constantly send and receive information. But the way we communicate has changed dramatically over time. Today we'll follow the journey of communication media from signal fires and drums, to paper and mail, to the telephone, radio and television, and finally the internet.

We'll learn four stages: communication before telecommunications, the beginning of telecommunications, mass communication, and the development of the internet — each stage has its own people and inventions.

This lesson is based on the ICT textbook, pages 46 to 47, and it's the foundation the next lessons on forms of communication will build on.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Assuming communication can happen without a transmission medium — no, any communication needs a sender, a medium and a receiver.

Confusing one-to-one sending with broadcasting to a large number — each has its own form we'll learn.

Imagining technology appeared all at once — no, each invention appeared in a stage and built on the one before it.

Communication Before Telecommunications

how did people communicate in ancient times?

### No electricity, no phone — so how?

In ancient times there was no phone, no text messages and no internet. People needed to get simple information across, and they used the easiest things available: signal fires or beating drums. After writing was developed, they wrote messages on paper and delivered them using messengers or carrier pigeons.

These are three stations — move with the arrows and watch the journey step by step.

Ancient times

Signal fires and drums

The first means of communication were visual signals like fires, or sound signals like drums — to get simple information to a small number of people nearby.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Not every old medium has the same range — signal fires and drums have a very short range, while paper and mail have a longer one.

Confusing the carrier of the information with its source — the carrier pigeon transports the message, it doesn't write it.

The modern postal system was established in 1840 — not in the same era as signal fires.

The Beginning of Telecommunications

inventions that changed the world in the 19th century

### Telecommunications: sending information remotely

Telecommunications means sending information from one place to another using technology. It all started with consecutive inventions in the 19th century: the telegraph, the telephone, the wireless, and then the cellular phone.

1837

Morse and the telegraph

An American named Morse invented the telegraph — a device for sending and receiving codes (like Morse code) through telecommunications.

1876

Bell and the telephone

In 1876, the American inventor Bell invented the telephone — the first medium that lets your voice reach another person in another place.

1895

Marconi and the wireless telegraph

In 1895, the Italian Marconi invented the wireless telegraph — it sends signals without wires, and that's what later developed into radios.

1946

Cellular phones

In 1946 the mobile (cellular) phone service began to be used in the United States — the beginning of the mobile phone in your hand today.

### Try it: how does Morse code work?

The telegraph distinguished a short signal (a dot) from a long signal (a dash). The SOS call code is: 3 dots, 3 dashes, 3 dots.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Confusing the inventors — Morse made the telegraph, Bell made the telephone, and Marconi made the wireless.

Morse is not the inventor of the wireless telegraph — that's Marconi, and it happened years after Morse.

The cellular phone began to be used in 1946 in the United States — not in Europe.

Mass Communication and Its Development

information reaches a large number at once

### Mass communication: one speaks, everyone hears

Mass communication (also called mass media) = a means of sending information to a very large number of people at the same time. The core idea: sending from one source to a large audience — one-way transmission from sender to receiver, not a two-way conversation.

The development of mass communication — from the printing press to television

Mid-15th century

Gutenberg and the movable-type printing press

The German inventor Gutenberg invented the movable-type printing press, making it easy to reproduce huge amounts of information — newspapers and books were published and information reached a large number of people.

1906

The first experimental radio broadcast

In 1906 the first experimental radio broadcast was conducted in the United States — radio started delivering sound to tens of thousands at the same moment.

1928

The first experimental television broadcast

In 1928 the first experimental television broadcast was conducted in the United States — image and sound together, and that's the beginning of the television we know.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Mass communication is usually one-way (sender to receiver) — not a two-way conversation.

Gutenberg invented the printing press, not the television — and that happened centuries before television.

The chronological order: radio (1906) came before television (1928).

The Development of the Internet

from a small experiment to a network connecting the whole world

### The internet started as ARPANET

The internet in front of you today started as a small experiment in 1969 called ARPANET, supported by the United States Department of Defense. At first the network was used by research institutions, then it evolved, became known as the internet, and began to spread among the general public in the 1990s.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

The internet wasn't built fully from the start — it began as an experiment called ARPANET in 1969.

ARPANET was built with the support of the US Department of Defense, not a private company.

The wide spread of the internet among the public happened in the 1990s, not from the moment of invention.

Recap — The Lesson Journey

what we covered in a few lines

1. Before telecommunications

Signal fires and drums → paper and messengers → the modern postal system (1840).

2. The beginning of telecommunications

Morse and the telegraph (1837) → Bell and the telephone (1876) → Marconi and the wireless (1895) → the cellular phone (1946).

3. Mass communication

The printing press (Gutenberg) → radio (1906) → television (1928): information to a large number at once, one direction.

4. The development of the internet

The ARPANET experiment (1969) → research institutions → the global network that spread among the public in the 1990s.

### Today's Message

Every medium along the journey was invented to solve one problem: information needs to get through. From fires and drums, to paper and mail, to the telephone, radio and television, all the way to the internet — the range grew longer, the speed grew higher, and the number of people reached grew larger.
