Lesson introduction — types of data and analysis

Data is everywhere — numbers, names, colors, and times. Today we learn to distinguish quantitative from qualitative data, know the four measurement scales, and then the data analysis approaches: descriptive and inferential statistics.

Think of it this way:

Quantitative data is what we can measure, like weight and age, and qualitative data describes categories like blood type and prefecture.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Confusing interval with ratio — interval scale: the differences between numbers have meaning (time, temperature), while ratio scale: both differences and ratios have meaning (length, weight, age).

Confusing quantitative with qualitative — quantitative is numbers with meaning, and qualitative classifies and distinguishes types.

Quantitative Data

numeric data with meaning — book page 123

Quantitative data is data expressed in numbers, where the size of the numbers has meaning.

It is divided into two scales: the interval scale and the ratio scale.

Think of it this way:

30° is better than 20° by a difference of 10 degrees (intervals). A weight of 60 kg is double 30 kg (ratios).

the scales of quantitative data

quantitative — numbers with meaningful size

interval — the differences between numbers

ratio — both differences and ratios

Qualitative Data

data that distinguishes classifications and types — book page 123

Qualitative data is data used to distinguish classifications or types.

It is divided into two scales: the nominal scale and the ordinal scale.

Think of it this way:

Blood type (nominal) only distinguishes categories, while student ranking in a test (ordinal) has meaningful order but not meaningful differences.

Descriptive Statistics

summarizing and organizing data — book page 123

Descriptive statistics is summarizing and organizing historical data to get insights about the characteristics of a sample.

It contains two types of measures: measures of central tendency and measures of spread.

Think of it this way:

Central tendency tells you the middle — mean, median, and mode. Spread tells you how scattered the numbers are — range, variance, and standard deviation.

Inferential Statistics

inference and prediction from sample data — book page 123

Inferential statistics is used to make inferences, predictions, and generalizations about the entire population from the data of a sample.

Its tools include hypothesis testing and confidence intervals.

Think of it this way:

We take a sample of school students and predict the satisfaction rate across the whole school — and we compute confidence intervals to know how accurate the prediction is.
