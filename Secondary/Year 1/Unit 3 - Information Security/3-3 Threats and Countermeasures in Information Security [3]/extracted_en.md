# Threats and Countermeasures [3]

Fraudulent Billing and One-Click Fraud

Scams That Cost You Money

### What's the difference between the two scams?

Many scams try to take your money unfairly — click each card to learn them:

Fraudulent billing

A scam in which a person is billed for a fictitious service they have never used, with the intent to fraudulently extract money.

💡 Like a bill arriving for a service you never even knew about.

One-click fraud

A scam where clicking a URL in a website or email automatically triggers a message pretending a contract has been concluded, followed by an exorbitant payment demand.

💡 A single click on a link can create a fake contract and demand money!

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Confusing them — fraudulent billing is a bill for a service never used, while one-click fraud is a contract triggered by merely clicking a URL.

Clicking links you are unsure about in strange websites or emails — that's how one-click fraud starts.

Unauthorized Acquisition of Information

Phishing and Data Theft

### Phishing and Skimming

Two dangerous ways to steal your information — click each card:

Phishing

A scam that uses fake websites disguised as financial institutions or public agencies to steal personal information such as PINs or account details.

💡 Example: an email posing as your bank leads you to a fake site asking for your card number.

Skimming

The act of illegally extracting data from someone's credit or debit card and using it to create a counterfeit card.

💡 Like a small device attached to an ATM that reads card data without you knowing.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Confusing phishing and spyware — phishing lures you to a fake website, while spyware collects your data from inside the computer.

Thinking any email asking for your data is genuine — real banks never ask for your PIN or password by email.

Social Engineering

Exploiting Human Psychology

### What is Social Engineering?

Social engineering is a method of fraudulently obtaining information by exploiting human psychology, carelessness, or lack of awareness.

There are 3 well-known forms — click each one:

Impersonation

The act of pretending to be someone else — such as making a phone call in their name — to obtain information.

💡 Example: someone calls pretending to be tech support to get your data.

Shoulder surfing

The act of peeking over someone's shoulder to steal passwords or PIN codes.

💡 Be careful when typing a PIN in public — someone might watch you.

Dumpster diving

The act of rummaging through trash to obtain discarded confidential information.

💡 Papers with your data must be shredded before being thrown away.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Thinking social engineering requires high tech — no, it mostly relies on deceiving people and exploiting their carelessness.

Forgetting phishing is not social engineering — phishing is a fake website, while social engineering exploits people themselves.

Information Security Policy

An Organization's Rules to Protect Information

### What is an Information Security Policy?

A set of fundamental rules and guidelines established by a company or organization to maintain and protect information security.

The policy is built on 3 levels — click each one:

Basic Policy

Describes the organization's fundamental principles and declarations regarding information security.

💡 The big picture — what the organization is committed to regarding protection.

Security Standards

Set out the rules for putting the basic policy into practice.

💡 Exactly how to apply the principles — through detailed standards and rules.

Implementation Procedures and Operational Rules

Clearly define the necessary procedures based on the target audience and intended use.

💡 Who does what and when — the final practical steps.
