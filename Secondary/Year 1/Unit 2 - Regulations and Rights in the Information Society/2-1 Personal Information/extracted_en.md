# Personal Information

What is Personal Information?

Core Definition

Personal information is any data or information related to a natural person who can be identified directly or indirectly by linking it with other data.

Examples of personal information: name, address, date of birth, gender, phone number, national ID number, passport number, driving license, or any data that identifies psychological, health, or economic identity.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Thinking personal information is only your name and address — no, any data that identifies you counts.

Thinking personal information only concerns living individuals — yes, information about deceased people is not personal information.

Types of Personal Information

Main Categories

### Main Classifications of Personal Information

Personal information has important classifications you should know to understand how it's protected:

Four Basic Items

Name, address, date of birth, and gender — the basic items also used for identity verification.

💡 Example: a passport includes name and date of birth as basic items for identity verification.

Personal Identification Codes

Numbers in passports, driver's licenses, or National IDs — just a sequence of digits that still identifies the person.

💡 Even though they're just numbers, they're personal information because they identify the individual.

Special Care-Required Personal Information

Information that requires careful handling to prevent prejudice and disadvantages — e.g., race, creed, social status, medical history, and criminal record.

💡 This is the most sensitive information — never share without the owner's consent.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Thinking a driver's license or National ID is just digits and not personal information — no, it identifies the individual when combined with other data.

Sharing medical history or criminal record with anyone — this is Special Care-Required Personal Information, prohibited without consent.

Act on the Protection of Personal Information

Proper Data Handling

### What is the Act?

The Act on the Protection of Personal Information stipulates the proper handling of personal information. It states that one cannot provide an individual's personal information to a third party without the individual's consent.

But in certain cases the Act permits provision without consent — click each case:

### 1. Based on laws and regulations

When a specific law or regulation requires the provision.

### 2. For national security or public interest

When necessary for the protection of national security or public interest.

### 3. To protect human life, body, or property

When necessary for the protection of human life, body, or property.

### 4. For public health or children's development

When especially necessary for public health or the healthy development of children.

### 5. Cooperating with the government

When cooperating with national or local governments, etc.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Thinking the Act prohibits providing personal information in all cases — no, in these five cases it is permitted without consent.

Thinking posting someone's economic or health status to aid a criminal investigation is not a violation — no, it's protected information and a violation of the Act.

Privacy and Image Rights

Your Basic Rights

### Three Important Rights

When we talk about privacy protection, there are 3 main rights — click each right to learn it:

Right to Privacy

The right of an individual to protect personal information that the individual does not want others to know.

💡 No one can force you to reveal your private information.

Image Rights

The right of an individual to prevent others from photographing or using their face or appearance without permission.

💡 You must not photograph or post someone's photo without permission — even family members.

Publicity Rights

A right that protects the economic interests of celebrities in their likeness, among other things.

💡 You must not print a celebrity's photo on a T-shirt and sell it for profit.

💡 Important note: these rights are recognized not by law but through court precedents.

Protection in Corporations and Organizations

Policies & Licenses

### Companies must protect your data

Corporations and organizations have specific tools to ensure personal information protection — click each tool:

Privacy Policy

A policy that stipulates how companies and organizations manage personal information.

💡 Read it before registering on any site to know what they do with your data.

Personal Data Protection Center License

The license obtained by companies from the regulatory authority (Personal Data Protection Center) to ensure compliance with the law.

💡 A company holding this license applies real protection measures to your data.

Prior Approval System

A method in which a provider does not collect user data or offer a service unless prior consent is given.

💡 'No consent = no service' — everything starts with your approval.

Objection System

A method in which a service provider continues to offer the service until the user requests it to be discontinued.

💡 Difference: here the service starts automatically and you request its discontinuation.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Confusing the Prior Approval System and Objection System — the first starts with your consent, the second starts automatically and you request its stop.

Thinking any company can collect your data without a license — no, companies need a license from the Personal Data Protection Center.
