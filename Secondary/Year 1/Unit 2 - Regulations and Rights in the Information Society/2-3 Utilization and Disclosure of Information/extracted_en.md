Utilization and Disclosure of Information

Balance Between Use and Protection

After learning about copyrights in the previous lesson, the important question now is: how can we use others' works legally without violating their rights? Here come concepts like fair use, exceptions, quotation rules, and Creative Commons licenses.

Purpose of Copyright and Exceptions

Fair Use

### Purpose of Copyright

A copyright aims to contribute to cultural development by ensuring the fair use of copyrighted works and the protection of rights.

Copyright exceptions — click each exception:

Reproduction for private use

You may record content for your own personal use.

💡 Example: recording a music CD you bought onto your smartphone for personal listening — not infringement.

Reproduction within educational institutions

Use of copyrighted works in the course of teaching within educational institutions.

💡 Example: copying materials and distributing to students in class — allowed.

Non-profit performances

Performances not for profit where no admission fee is charged.

💡 Be careful: if you charge an admission fee, it becomes infringement! Like a band concert charging entry.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Thinking copying a commercial CD and distributing it to friends is personal use — no, that's copyright infringement.

Thinking charging an admission fee for a performance doesn't matter — no, paid performances are not exempt.

Quotation Rules

Legal Partial Use

### What is a quotation?

Quotation is the act of using a portion of another person's copyrighted work in your own copyrighted work. If certain requirements are met, portions of a copyrighted work can be quoted without obtaining permission from the author.

5 quotation rules — click each rule:

### 1. Your work takes precedence

Your own copyrighted work takes precedence, while the quoted work is subordinate. (Your own copyrighted work is the subject.)

### 2. Necessity to quote

There must be a necessity to engage in quotation.

### 3. Clearly identifiable quotation

Ensure that the quoted material is clearly identifiable by enclosing it in quotation marks.

### 4. Specify the source

The source is clearly specified.

### 5. Do not alter

Do not alter the quoted material.

Protection Duration and Creative Commons Licenses

Economic Rights and Licenses

### Duration of Protection

The economic rights of the author expire 50 years after the death of the creator. After that, the work enters the public domain and can be used freely.

### Creative Commons Licenses

A Creative Commons License (CC License) is a mark indicating the conditions for using copyrighted works. The creator chooses the conditions — click each one:

Credit to creator (BY)

Display the title of the work and name of the creator.

💡 Any use must credit the creator.

Non-commercial (NC)

Do not use for commercial purposes.

💡 You must not sell or profit from using the work.

No derivatives (ND)

Do not alter the original copyrighted work.

💡 Use it as-is only, without changing it.

Same terms for adaptations (SA)

Publish under the same combination of licenses as the original work.

💡 If you adapt the work, the modified version must follow the same license.

### Test yourself: did you understand the licenses?

A work requiring: display the creator's name and the work's title

That's the BY condition (Credit to creator)

✅ Correct — that's BY

A work requiring: don't use it for commercial purposes

That's the NC condition (Non-commercial)

❌ Wrong — that's NC, not BY

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Mixing up the license conditions — BY = credit, NC = non-commercial, ND = no derivatives, SA = same terms for adaptations.

Thinking licenses remove copyrights entirely — no, licenses define usage conditions and don't erase the creator's right.

Which statement correctly describes quotation methods and rules from A to D?

A  There is a necessity to quote, and the portion being cited is subordinate

B  Even when quoting, it is always necessary to obtain permission from the creator

C  Regardless of the reason, you must not modify the quoted material

D  It is not necessary to identify the quoted material

When publishing a photo you took on a web page, you want to allow others to use it on the conditions that 'the photographer's name and title are displayed' and 'the work is not altered' — which licenses should you combine?

A  NC + SA

B  BY + ND

C  BY + NC

D  ND + SA

✅ Answer: B — BY represents display (credit) and ND represents no derivatives (no alteration). (NC means non-commercial and SA means share-alike, so the other combinations don't match the two required conditions.)
