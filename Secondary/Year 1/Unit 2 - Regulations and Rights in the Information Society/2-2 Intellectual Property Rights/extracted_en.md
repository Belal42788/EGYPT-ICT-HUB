# Intellectual Property Rights

What are Intellectual Property Rights?

Core Definition

Intellectual Property Rights are rights that protect creations and ideas generated by human intellectual activities. These rights are mainly divided into Industrial Property Rights and Copyrights.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Thinking IP rights protect ideas themselves — no, they protect the expression of ideas in tangible creations.

Thinking these rights are one thing — no, they divide into two main branches: industrial and copyrights.

Industrial Property Rights

The Formality Principle

### What are Industrial Property Rights?

Industrial Property Rights are rights primarily related to the manufacturing of industrial or commercial products. This follows the principle in which rights come into effect once an application is filed with and registered by the Egypt Patent Office. This is known as the Formality Principle.

Types of Industrial Property Rights — click each type:

Patent Rights

Rights to advanced technological ideas and inventions.

📅 Protection period: 20 years from the application

Utility Model Rights

Rights to the shape or structure of a product.

📅 Protection period: 7 years from the application

Design Rights

Rights to a design, such as the shape and patterns of a product.

📅 Protection period: 10 years from the application (renewed once every 5 years)

Trademark Rights

Rights related to items such as product names, logos, text, and sound.

📅 Protection period: 10 years from the registration (renewable)

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Mixing up protection periods — patent 20 years, utility model 7, design 10, trademark 10 from registration.

Thinking industrial rights are automatic without registration — no, they follow the Formality Principle and registration.

Copyrights

The Non-Formality Principle

### What are Copyrights?

Copyrights are rights related to creative activities in the arts, covering works such as novels, movies, paintings, photographs, music, and computer programs. It follows the Non-Formality Principle, under which rights are established the moment a copyrighted work is created, regardless of whether the creator is an amateur or a minor.

The Copyright Act is a law that protects the rights of creators.

Rights of the creator — click each right:

### 1. Moral rights of creator

Rights that protect the personal interests of the creator.

### 2. Copyrights (property rights)

Rights that protect the economic interests of the creator.

Other important concepts — click each one:

Neighboring Rights

Rights that arise for individuals or entities who communicate or transmit copyrighted works.

💡 Granted to performers such as singers, directors, and actors, as well as record companies and broadcasting organizations.

Copyright protection period

The lifetime of the creator plus 50 years after their death.

💡 Works of creators dead for over 50 years fall into the public domain.

Copyright infringement

Using a work without permission constitutes copyright infringement.

💡 You must obtain permission from the creator before reproducing their work or publishing it on a website.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Thinking copyrights protect the quality of a work — no, rights apply to the work itself, not its quality.

Thinking works by creators like Taha Hussein or Beethoven are protected forever — no, after 50 years from death they enter the public domain, but performers' and record companies' neighboring rights remain.

Downloading music or movies for sale while knowing they were illegally uploaded — that's copyright infringement even for personal use.
