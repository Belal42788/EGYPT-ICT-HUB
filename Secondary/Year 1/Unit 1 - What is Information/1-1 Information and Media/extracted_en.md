# 1-1 Information and Media


## Basic Hierarchy: Data ← Information ← Knowledge

Data, Information, Knowledge

### Our Goal Today

Understand that data, information, and knowledge are stages on a pyramid, and each stage adds value to the one before it — not just different names.

### Data

Facts or matters represented using numbers, characters, or symbols — without context.

### Information

Something that holds meaning or value for the recipient and serves as a basis for decision-making. Unlike objects, information has no form.

### Knowledge

Information that has been analyzed and systematized to aid in problem-solving.

Step 1

### Data — Raw Numbers

This is what a hospital records first: readings like 37.5, 120/80, and 98%. Numbers alone, with no one explaining them — no meaning yet.

Step 2

### Information — Context Added

When the doctor reads 37.5 as body temperature and 98% as oxygen level — the numbers now have meaning and become a basis for a decision. That is Information.

Step 3

### Knowledge — Analysis & Pattern

When numbers are analyzed together and a pattern appears: high temperature + low oxygen = likely infection. This pattern gives a treatment plan — that is Knowledge.

### Quick Recap

Like the pyramid going up: Data gains context to become Information, and Information gets analyzed to become Knowledge. Each stage is extra value.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Thinking Knowledge is just more information — no, it is analysis and patterns, not extra quantity.

Believing data and information are the same thing — the difference is the context that adds meaning.

## Information Characteristics

Persistence, Reproducibility, Propagation

### Our Goal Today

Understand the three characteristics of digital information, and match a real scenario to each one.

### The Three Characteristics of Information

### Persistence

No matter how hard you try, information can never be completely erased. Like the photo you deleted but found in a backup.

### Reproducibility

Information can be copied in large quantities without degradation. A file copied 1000 times stays identical to the original.

### Propagation

Information is easy to convey and spread. A tweet can reach millions of people in seconds through mass media and the internet.

### Summarize in words

Persistence: it won't be erased. Reproducibility: it copies easily. Propagation: it spreads fast.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Confusing Persistence with Propagation: Persistence means it stays, Propagation means it spreads fast. Not the same thing.

Forgetting that copies keep exactly the same quality — digital information does not lose quality when copied.

## Primary vs Secondary Information

Primary vs Secondary Information

### Our Goal Today

Understand the difference between primary and secondary information, and how the classification is relative to who is doing the checking.

### Primary Information

Information obtained through direct personal experience, or acquired through research and experiments you conducted yourself.

### Examples

Lab experiment reports, survey results, tabulations of questionnaire results.

### Secondary Information

Information not obtained directly by yourself, but acquired through a third party.

### Examples

Books, newspapers, television.

### Cross-checking

Secondary information can sometimes differ from the original or have interpretations added to it. So we must compare the obtained information from multiple perspectives with other information — that is cross-checking.

Forgetting that secondary information can be distorted or interpreted by a third party — that's why we cross-check.

## Media Types

Expression, Transmission, Recording

### Our Goal Today

Track how information travels through three media types: expression, transmission, and recording — and what each one does.

### The Three Media Types

### Expression media

A means of expressing information: text, images, audio, video.

How we express

### Transmission media

A medium for transmission and communication: TV, radio, newspapers, telephone, internet.

How it gets there

### Recording media

A medium for recording and storing: paper, USB drives, DVDs, cloud storage.

### Key point

Is a book one medium only? No — a book has text (expression), reaches you (transmission), and stores info (recording). One medium can play more than one role.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Not seeing recording media as separate from transmission — a book, for example, does both: it transmits and records.

Thinking audio and video are transmission media — no, they are expression media; transmission happens via radio or internet.

## Media Literacy

Media Literacy

### Our Goal Today

Start the habit of pausing before sharing: the source? the evidence? other sources? — to evaluate information correctly.

### What is Media Literacy?

It is the ability to understand the meaning and characteristics of media, and to accurately interpret the information coming from it — not accepting everything without questioning.
